# Light Reflection and Refraction

## Reflection
- Reflection is light bouncing back from a surface.
- Laws of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal all lie in the same plane.
- A plane mirror forms an image that is virtual, upright, the same size as the object, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

## Spherical mirrors
- A concave mirror curves inwards. It can form real or virtual images, and is used in torches, shaving mirrors and by dentists.
- A convex mirror curves outwards. It always forms a virtual, upright, smaller image, and gives a wide view, so it is used as a vehicle's rear-view mirror.
- The focal length is half the radius of curvature: f = R/2.
- Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. Magnification m = -v/u.

## Refraction
- Refraction is light bending when it passes from one medium to another, because its speed changes.
- Going from air into glass, light slows down and bends towards the normal.
- Refractive index = speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed of light in the medium. A higher value means an optically denser medium.
- Everyday examples: a pencil looks bent in water, and a pool looks shallower than it is.

## Lenses
- A convex lens is thicker in the middle and converges light. It is used in magnifying glasses and cameras.
- A concave lens is thinner in the middle and diverges light.
- Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f.
- Power of a lens P = 1/f, with f in metres. The unit is the dioptre (D). Convex lenses have positive power; concave lenses have negative power.

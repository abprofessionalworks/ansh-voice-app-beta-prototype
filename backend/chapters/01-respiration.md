# Respiration

## What respiration is
- Respiration is the process that releases energy from food, mainly glucose, inside living cells.
- It happens in every living cell, all the time. It is different from breathing, which only moves air in and out of the lungs.
- The energy released is stored as ATP, the cell's energy currency.

## Aerobic respiration
- Happens when oxygen is available.
- Glucose is first broken down into pyruvate in the cytoplasm.
- Pyruvate is then broken down further inside the mitochondria.
- Products: carbon dioxide, water and a large amount of energy.

## Anaerobic respiration
- Happens without oxygen and releases much less energy.
- In yeast: pyruvate becomes ethanol (alcohol) and carbon dioxide. This is called fermentation and is used in making bread rise.
- In muscle cells during hard exercise: when oxygen runs short, pyruvate becomes lactic acid. Lactic acid building up causes muscle cramps.

## Breathing in humans
- Air enters through the nostrils, where fine hairs and mucus filter dust.
- It passes through the pharynx, larynx and trachea (windpipe), then into the bronchi and bronchioles.
- It ends in tiny air sacs called alveoli. Their thin walls and rich blood supply allow oxygen to enter the blood and carbon dioxide to leave.
- Rings of cartilage keep the trachea from collapsing.
- Breathing in: the diaphragm flattens and the ribs move up and out, so the chest gets bigger and air rushes in.
- Haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen to the body.

## Respiration in plants
- Plants exchange gases through stomata in leaves and lenticels in stems.
- During the day, the oxygen from photosynthesis is more than plants use in respiration, so they give out oxygen overall.
- At night, with no photosynthesis, plants give out carbon dioxide.
